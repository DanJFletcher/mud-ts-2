# Continuity, chronology, and geography

## Time

Choose a chronology already used in the wiki. Do not create a new era numbering system without need. If dates are uncertain, use explicit phrasing such as "early Fifth Age, according to the coastal annals" instead of assigning false precision.

For each event, verify: preceding cause, people alive at the time, government then in power, actual location name in that era, credible aftermath and current historical interpretation. Do not refer to a faction as a pre-existing participant if its founding is later than the event.

## People and kinship

Check birth/death years, titles, family relations, succession and membership dates. A parent cannot inherit a title from a child at an earlier date; a deceased figure may influence later history but cannot personally attend an event after their death unless the setting explicitly establishes it.

Differentiate legal, ceremonial, claimed, former, and self-proclaimed titles. A historic alias does not make two figures identical.

## Places

Keep region containment (town in district in nation) consistent; distinguish political boundaries from natural geography. When writing road, river or sea relationships, verify upstream/downstream and neighboring directions against existing maps/articles before asserting distances. Do not invent coordinates, routes or metric travel times for wiki completeness.

## Contradiction handling

1. Find the two conflicting statements and their source authority.
2. Decide whether they truly refer to the same people, place and era.
3. If the apparent conflict comes from different in-world witnesses, document both as attributed competing accounts.
4. If it is an editorial mistake, correct the lower-authority draft and linked pages.
5. If conflicting approved canon cannot be resolved from sources, avoid selecting one silently. Keep the disputed point unresolved in the affected draft article, without publishing invented certainty.

## Historiography and disputed truth

Distinguish *what happened*, *what contemporary people recorded*, and *what later generations believe*. A canonical legend does not imply a canonical supernatural event. Pages may have "Traditional accounts" and "Scholarly interpretations" sections when credible in-world sources are known or newly drafted at user request.
