# Writing style: compelling reference prose

Write as a careful editor who knows the setting, not as a quest narrator or salesperson. First paragraphs should define the subject clearly and establish the most important context. Give readers an accurate mental model before literary flourish.

## Page cadence

- Lead: 2–4 sentences identifying what the subject is and why it matters to the world.
- Body: usually 2–5 meaningful sections for a typical article; long pages get a succinct lead and cross-links.
- Close: related articles; no game tasks, "adventure ideas", "GM secrets", stat block or reward table.
- Technical editorial metadata belongs in frontmatter, not paragraphs.

## Fictional histories that feel lived in

Give places functional identities (why settlement arose, roads/rivers, common building material, food, markets, changed borders and migration). Give people nuance (public acts, family or mentorship, contradiction, losses, shifts in allegiance and the viewpoints of others). Give cultures internal diversity and history (community institutions, work, languages, celebrations and dissent) rather than one universal trait.

A historic calamity can have multiple interpretations: describe the official chronicle, folk memory, and scholars' doubts, with appropriate attribution. Never write "the truth is..." unless that truth is both authorized and appropriate for the audience tier.

## Sentence-level craft

- Prefer proper nouns that are easy to distinguish.
- Use measured specificity: "slate roofs built after the fires of 412" is stronger than "ancient mysterious buildings" when 412 is supported or newly authored draft lore.
- Avoid repeating stock adjectives such as ancient, enigmatic, shadowy, fabled and mysterious to manufacture depth.
- Do not flatten all peoples into absolute statements. Use "many families in the southern coves" rather than "all coastfolk" when diversity is plausible.
- Keep temporal tense consistent; an entity may change its name or boundaries over time.
- Explain local terms at first mention and link a glossary/related page when one exists.
- Use in-world voice in a clearly attributed quotation or dedicated manuscript entry, rather than as an unexplained omniscient editorial narrator.

## This is not quest design

Bad (out of scope): "Players must visit the abbey, talk to the prior, and recover three bells." 

Good (in scope): "The abbey held three ceremonial bells until the western tower collapsed in the winter of 488. Records differ on whether the third was recovered." 

Bad (out of scope): "The blade grants +2 to attacks and begins the smith's quest." 

Good (in scope): "The blade's sea-glass hilt made it a symbol of the coastal succession dispute; later owners sometimes concealed it for fear of royal confiscation."

Avoid exploratory "hooks" written as instructions. Distinctive unresolved history is fine, but not an implicit set of player steps.
