The Returning Tide describes reality as passing through recurring cycles of creation and dissolution. In its central account, Namar, the Remembering One, persists across cycles. What counts as persistence—memory, identity, or a name carried forward—is interpreted differently among its communities.

Stillwater Orders accept impermanence and the natural cycles of existence. High-Tide Pilgrims emphasize preserving knowledge and civilization across cycles. Their difference is not simply resignation versus action: both traditions make room for care, preparation, and grief, while disagreeing about what can endure and what should be carried.

Rituals often draw on water, repeated readings, and the keeping of records, though local forms vary. The tradition's cycle is a religious account, not a confirmed astronomical process. Its teaching that Namar remembers does not settle the disputed nature of [[sprites|sprite memory]] or consciousness.
