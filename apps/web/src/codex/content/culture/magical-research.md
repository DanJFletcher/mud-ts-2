Reliable study of [[resonance|Resonance]] requires a record of the pattern used, materials, energy, scale, duration, and conditions of observation. Repeating an effect under similar conditions can show whether a result is dependable; it does not necessarily explain why the effect exists.

Researchers compare measurements, report failed attempts, and distinguish direct observation from interpretation. Instruments may reveal one kind of change while missing another. A witness's account, a mechanical reading, and a sprite's own explanation should not be treated as interchangeable evidence.

The [[glass-collegium|Glass Collegium]] provides one institutional setting for such work. [[elira-venn|Elira Venn's measurements]] of the Silent Bell illustrate both the value of careful records and the danger of assuming that a sequence of events proves causation.
