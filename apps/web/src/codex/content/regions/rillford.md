Rillford is a settlement of approximately 620 people, about a day's travel southwest of [[bellwold|Bellwold]] along the river corridor provisionally called the [[lower-rill|Lower Rill]]. It is the largest established civilian settlement in the immediate region. The town is part of the [[crown-of-heskar|Crown of Heskar]] and functions as a crossing and commercial center.

## The crossing and its trade

Merchant guilds, customs officials, inns, warehouses, and a weekly livestock market cluster around the crossing. River transport carries goods that would be costly or slow to move by cart, but passage depends on water conditions, agreements, and the people operating the boats. A ferry guild seeks exclusive transport rights; independent boatmen oppose the proposal. Guildmistress [[selda-rook|Selda Rook]] supports regulated river commerce, though she does not speak for every merchant or boat crew.

Rillford's position links it with Bellwold, [[harrowfen|Harrowfen]], and communities farther along the river. Its commercial influence is substantial, but no regional tariff schedule or universal right of passage is established.

## The river and its residents

The town's riverside work includes loading, repairs, animal handling, and watching for changes in the current. The name Lower Rill is provisional; the river's full course and any formal connection to the [[rillward-freeholds|Rillward Freeholds]] remain unsettled.

A river sprite called [[old-silverfin|Old Silverfin]] is associated with the crossing. Boat crews say it has begun overturning vessels carrying certain cargo. The reports do not agree on what the cargoes have in common or what the sprite intends. Some residents regard the incidents as a warning; others suspect unsafe loading or deliberate interference. No account has been verified.

The archive gives no founding date for Rillford or the crossing. The current town grew around river passage, warehouses, and the weekly market. Residents follow different religious traditions and practical philosophies: some treat the river as a presence deserving respect, while others describe it chiefly through current, weather, and the work of safe passage. Neither view speaks for the whole town.

Rillford's population, travel time, guild dispute, and reported sprite activity are provisional regional canon. Its relative size is the reason Bellwold is described as an important local market rather than the region's largest town.
