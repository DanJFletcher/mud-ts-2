Ashcombe is a settlement of approximately 210 woodcutters, charcoal burners, carpenters, and cartwrights, about a day's travel northwest of [[bellwold|Bellwold]]. It is within the [[crown-of-heskar|Crown of Heskar]]. Its timber and charcoal support building and metalworking across the region.

## Work and authority

Ashcombe's work is organized around felling, hauling, seasoning, burning, and shaping wood. Timber yards and charcoal kilns sit near the settlement, with access to forest stands determined by a mix of permits, older practice, and local arrangements. Heskar officials have recently issued larger concessions to commercial interests. Workers and nearby communities dispute which stands those grants can legitimately include.

Carpenter [[oren-kest|Oren Kest]] refuses timber taken from certain areas. His reasons combine practical knowledge of the woods with a reluctance to treat every mapped stand as an available resource. Other residents support the concessions as reliable work; some fear that a short-term increase in orders will leave fewer trees for future households.

No founding date or single founding account is recorded. The settlement's present form reflects the work of timber yards, kilns, and the roads used to move heavy loads. Religious practice varies among households; some people interpret unusual signs in the forest through local beliefs about sprites, while others regard such accounts as untested explanations.

## A disputed account

Workers felled an ancient oak whose cut surface appeared to weep clear sap. Afterward, some reported hearing voices in harvested lumber. Several believe the tree housed a sprite; others describe the sounds as settling timber, stress, or rumor amplified by a difficult season. The tree's nature and the reports' cause are unverified.

The dispute has made Ashcombe's relationship with the [[uncrowned-lands|Uncrowned Lands]] more immediate. People who use nearby woods may recognize boundaries and obligations that do not appear on Heskar concession maps. The proposed [[road-through-old-ground|Road Through Old Ground]] raises similar questions about timber access and who can authorize changes to forest land.

Ashcombe's population, travel time, and economic links are provisional. Its residents include both supporters and critics of commercial logging; neither position represents the whole settlement.
