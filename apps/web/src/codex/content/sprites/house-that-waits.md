The House That Waits is an often-cited account of a sprite associated with a dwelling. Visitors describe a presence that seems attentive to the people who enter and leave, though the surviving descriptions do not agree about how the house communicates.

The account raises a practical question: if the sprite is tied to a structure, what does it mean to own, repair, sell, or abandon the house? A building's legal deed cannot by itself establish the wishes of a being associated with it.

The story is treated as a record of an unusual case, not proof that every old house contains a sprite. It is frequently discussed alongside [[sprite-anchoring|anchoring]] and [[sprite-law-and-culture|local sprite law]].
