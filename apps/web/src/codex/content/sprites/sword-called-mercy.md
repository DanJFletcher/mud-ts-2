The Sword Called Mercy is an example of an embodied sprite associated with a weapon. Its name is often used to challenge the assumption that a tool-like form cannot hold a point of view.

Records disagree about whether the name is self-chosen, given by a former bearer, or translated from a form of communication that has no direct human equivalent. The surviving accounts do not establish the sword's full history, and this entry does not treat it as a generic example of every embodied sprite.

The ethical question is not only who may carry it, but whether the being can refuse a use, change its mind, or leave an anchor. Those questions belong to the wider discussion of [[sprite-consent-and-personhood|sprite consent and personhood]].
