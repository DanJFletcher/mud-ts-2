Harvests in [[harrowfen|Harrowfen]] have begun declining despite weather that residents describe as favorable. The village's farms supply food to [[bellwold|Bellwold]], where the shortfall has increased dependence on imported grain. No reliable account yet establishes how much of the change is local, how long it has lasted, or whether each crop is affected in the same way.

## Competing explanations

Farmers, landholders, and officials disagree about the cause. Some point to depleted soil or changes in drainage and field use. Others suspect a change in local [[resonance|Resonance]] or nearby [[veyl|Veyl]] extraction. These ideas have not been tested against a complete set of field records, and none is established as the explanation.

Reeve [[maela-thorn|Maela Thorn]] is negotiating for farmers who have different needs and levels of land ownership. Some want more control over cultivation; others prioritize immediate income or protection from debt. The village is not of one mind about what evidence should be collected or who should pay for it.

## A proposed bargain

A commercial interest has offered inexpensive grain in exchange for permission to establish a Veyl extraction operation. Supporters see a way to stabilize food supplies and bring income. Opponents question the consent and ecological costs, and whether a temporary bargain would make Harrowfen dependent on an operation whose effects remain uncertain.

The proposal connects the harvest dispute to a wider debate about medicine, trade, and extraction in the [[bellwold-marches|Bellwold Marches]]. It does not prove that Veyl caused the harvest decline. The cause, the proposal's sponsor, and the long-term consequences remain unresolved.
