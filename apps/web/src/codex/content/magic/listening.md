Listening studies detection, communication, and interactions with [[sprites|sprites]]. Its practitioners compare testimony, repeated observations, and changes in a sprite's surroundings. A signal interpreted as speech by one listener may be reported as pattern or sensation by another.

Listening does not guarantee truthful answers or make a sprite available for questioning. The practice is therefore inseparable from debates about [[sprite-consent-and-personhood|consent and personhood]], especially when institutions seek information or materials from a being with its own agency.

Methods vary widely, and the discipline's records are shaped by the interests of the people who fund and preserve them. Its name describes a field of practice rather than a claim that every sprite communicates in sound.
